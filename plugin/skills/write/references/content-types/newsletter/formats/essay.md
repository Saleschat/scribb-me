- One story, one point. The subject line and preview carry the point from the start, so a reader who opens is never lost.
- Open with the point, or a direct hello and the point. Don't hold the point back behind a suspense scene; tell stories as examples once the reader knows why they matter.
- Paragraphs may run to about 80 words when they tell a short story; paragraphs that explain stay at one to three sentences.
- A short essay needs no headings. A longer one uses 3–6 plain headings, often questions the reader would ask. No "In this issue" list; secondary items go in a P.S.
- Numbers and claims name their source in the sentence and link it.

**Subject:** Why we moved support to 7 am

**Preview:** A café owner's message at 6:52 changed our hours.

From Monday, someone at Foo answers support messages from 7 am, two hours earlier than before. Here's why.

Last Tuesday at 6:52, a café owner messaged us about a broken card reader. She had a queue out the door. We answered at nine, by which time she'd taken cash for two hours and turned away everyone who didn't have any.

We'd set our hours around our own day, not hers. Most of the businesses we work with open before we wake up.

[See the new support hours](https://example.com/support-hours)

Priya, Foo's head of support
