- One story, one point. The subject line and preview carry the point from the start, so a reader who opens is never lost.
- A scene may come before the point, but the first sentence must make sense on its own and the point must be clear by the end of the second paragraph.
- Story paragraphs may run to about 120 words when they tell one scene; paragraphs that explain stay at one to three sentences.
- No headings and no "In this issue" list. Secondary items go after the story in a P.S., or under at most one plain heading.
- Numbers and claims name their source in the sentence and link it.

**Subject:** Why we stopped answering at nine

**Preview:** A café owner's 6:52 am message changed our support hours.

Last Tuesday at 6:52 in the morning, a café owner sent us a message about a broken card reader. She had a queue out the door. We answered at nine.

By then she'd taken cash for two hours and turned away everyone who didn't have any. She told us this calmly, which was worse.

We'd set our hours around our own day, not hers. Most of the businesses we work with open before we wake up.

From Monday, someone answers from seven. [See the new support hours](https://example.com/support-hours)

Next Tuesday at 6:52, someone will be there.

Priya, Foo's head of support
