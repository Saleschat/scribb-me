# Newsletter

For people who send email to an audience that signed up: founders writing product updates, creators sending a weekly issue, teams welcoming new subscribers. The reader chose to get this email, and can stop at any time. Every line has to earn the next one.

Default freedom: balanced. A newsletter can have more personality than docs, but the conventions below still apply.

## One main point
- Decide the one thing the reader should take away before you write. If an issue has three equal headlines, it has none.
- Put that point in the subject line and in the first two sentences.
- Secondary items go below, in short sections, or wait for the next issue.

## Subject line and preview text
- Say what's inside: "Scheduled reports are here", "Five pricing pages we learned from".
- Aim for 30–50 characters, so it isn't cut off on a phone.
- No clickbait ("You won't believe…", "This changes everything"), no ALL CAPS, no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:". At most one emoji, and only if your newsletter always uses one.
- The preview text (the line shown after the subject in the inbox) adds a second reason to open. Don't repeat the subject, and don't leave it to the email client, which will show "View in browser".

## The opening
- Start with the news. The first sentence should make sense on its own if the reader stops there.
- Skip pleasantries and filler: `I hope this email finds you well`, `Happy Monday!`, `It's been a while since our last update`.
- Don't open by recapping the previous issue. Link to it if it matters.

## Voice
- Write to one reader, as "you". Use "I" or "we" for the sender, consistently.
- Sound like the person who signs it. Read it aloud: if you wouldn't say a sentence to a customer over coffee, rewrite it.
- Be specific instead of excited. "Reports now send themselves every Monday" beats `We're thrilled to announce a game-changing new feature`.
- Hype words wear out fast: `thrilled`, `beyond excited`, `revolutionary`, `huge news`. Let the news carry the weight.
- One exclamation mark is fine now and then. Stacked ones (`!!`, `?!`) and an exclamation mark on every line read as shouting.

## Structure
- Short paragraphs: one to three sentences.
- For an issue with several items, give each a descriptive heading ("New: scheduled reports", not "Update #2").
- Use bullets for lists of things, not for an argument.
- Put the most important item first. Readers drop off as they scroll.
- Keep it as long as the content needs. A product update is often 100–250 words; a regular issue can run to 800–1,200 if every section is worth it.

## Links and calls to action
- One clear call to action per section. Several competing buttons mean none gets clicked.
- Link text says where the link goes: "Read how scheduled reports work", not `click here` or `here`.
- Put the call to action right after the paragraph that makes the case for it.
- Say what happens after the click when it isn't obvious ("opens a 2-minute video").

## Closings and sign-offs
- End when the content ends. Don't add a closing paragraph that repeats what you just said.
- A sign-off with a real name and role builds trust: "Maya, Foo's product lead".
- Replace `Don't forget to…` with the thing itself: "Book your seat by Friday".
- Skip stock closings such as `As always, thanks for reading!` unless they're genuinely part of your voice. A plain "Thanks for reading" is fine.
- A short P.S. is read more than most body text. Use it for one useful extra, not for a second pitch.

## Plain text and accessibility
- Every issue should make sense as plain text: some readers and clients block images and HTML.
- Images need alt text that says what the image shows, and the point must not live only in an image.
- Don't rely on colour alone to show what's new or important.

## Formats
- `formats/regular-issue.md`: a recurring issue with a lead story and a few short sections.
- `formats/product-update.md`: one feature or change, why it matters, how to use it.
- `formats/welcome-email.md`: the first email a new subscriber gets.

## Before and after
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| `Subject: BIG NEWS!!! 🎉🎉` | Subject: Scheduled reports are here |
| `We're thrilled to announce our most exciting feature yet!` | You can now schedule a report from any dashboard. |
| `To learn more, click here.` | Read how scheduled reports work. |
| `Don't forget to share this with your team!` | Forward this to anyone who still screenshots dashboards. |

## Checker rules (conventions)
- `ScribbNewsletter.EmailCliches`: stock email openers such as `hope this email finds you well`.
- `ScribbNewsletter.HypeAnnouncements`: `thrilled to announce`, `excited to unveil` and similar.
- `ScribbNewsletter.LinkText`: link text such as `here` or `click here`.
- `ScribbNewsletter.ExclamationStack`: two or more exclamation marks in a row, or `?!`.
- `ScribbNewsletter.ExclamationCount`: more than two exclamation marks in one paragraph.

Subject lines, the one-main-point rule and closing recaps need judgement, so the reviewer checks those.
