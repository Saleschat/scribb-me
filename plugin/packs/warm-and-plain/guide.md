# Warm and plain

A style for products and docs that want to sound human without sounding like marketing. It reads like a helpful colleague: relaxed, clear and on the reader's side.

Good for: Docs and UI copy.

## Voice
- Conversational. Contractions are the default.
- "We" for the team and "you" for the reader. "We recommend" and "we'll email you" are fine.
- Kind, never cute. Warmth comes from being clear and considerate, not from exclamation marks or jokes.
- Admit limits plainly: "This can take a few minutes."

## Sentences
- Mix short and medium sentences. A sentence can start with "And" or "But".
- Give the reason when it helps someone decide, in a short clause: "Export your data first, so you have a copy."

## Words
- Everyday words: use, help, start, about, feature, set up.
- Avoid product jargon and stiff verbs: functionality, facilitate, commence, terminate, endeavour.
- Name what people care about: "your work", "your team", "your data".

## In docs
- Open with what the reader gets, in one friendly sentence.
- Keep steps as plain as any other style. Warmth goes in the intro and the transitions, not in step text.

## In UI copy
- Toasts can be a full short sentence: "Your project's been deleted."
- Errors explain gently and say what to do: "We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection and try again."

## Before and after
Before:
> Users cannot utilize this functionality until the configuration process has been completed.

After:
> You can use this feature once you've finished setting it up.

## Checker rules (suggestions)
`WarmAndPlain.Contractions` suggests contractions for "do not", "cannot", "it is", "you will", "we will" and similar. `WarmAndPlain.PlainWords` suggests everyday words for "functionality", "facilitate", "commence" and "terminate".
