# Short and plain

*Example style: who it sounds like. Taste layered on top of a content type; the content type wins on a conflict.*

A style for writing that gets read to the end. It sounds like someone who knows the subject and respects your time: short sentences, concrete words, strong verbs. Every sentence earns its place, and nothing is there to sound impressive.

Good for: Docs, website and sales pages, newsletters and emails.

## Sentences
- Keep most sentences under about 20 words. Mix in a three-word one now and then, but don't write a page of fragments.
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence has two "and"s and a "which", split it.
- Put the subject and the verb near the start. Say who does what.

> **Before:** The configuration of the export settings, which can be found under the Data tab, should be completed prior to the first sync being run.
>
> **After:** Set up export in **Data** before your first sync.

## Paragraphs
- One idea per paragraph. Three or four sentences is plenty; one is fine.
- Lead with the point. The reader should get it from the first sentence alone.
- End on the point too. Stop when you've said it; don't add a closing line that repeats it.

## Words
- Concrete nouns over abstract ones: "invoices", not "financial documentation"; "two days", not "a reduced timeframe".
- Strong verbs over a weak verb plus a noun: "decide", not "make a decision"; "review", not "carry out a review".
- Few adjectives and adverbs. If a verb needs an adverb, look for a better verb: "sprint", not "run quickly".
- Everyday words over formal ones: start, help, try, about, buy, end, use. Your reader knows them all already.

> **Before:** We endeavor to facilitate a seamless onboarding experience for every customer who purchases a plan.
>
> **After:** We help every new customer get set up in their first week.

## Cut the qualifiers
- Words like "very", "really", "quite", "somewhat", "extremely" and "rather" either add nothing or hide a vague claim. Cut them, or replace the claim with a fact.

> **Before:** The new importer is really quite fast, and it's extremely reliable.
>
> **After:** The new importer loads 10,000 rows in four seconds. It hasn't failed a run since March.

## Don't overdo it
- Short isn't the goal; clear is. Keep a long sentence when splitting it would break a cause from its effect.
- Keep a technical term when your reader expects it. "Idempotent" is plain to a developer.
- Keep warmth where the content type asks for it. Plain isn't cold.

## What the checker covers
- `ShortAndPlain.Qualifiers` suggests cutting very, really, quite, somewhat, extremely and rather (but not "rather than").
- `ShortAndPlain.PlainWords` suggests the everyday word for commence, facilitate, endeavor, approximately, purchase, terminate and assist.

Both are suggestions: a nudge, never a block. The reviewer covers sentence length, one idea per paragraph, concrete words and strong verbs.
